# Exceed Box — using it

For the Japan office and the Dubai desk. Nothing here needs a technical
background.

The whole idea, in one line: **you are told what to do next, you do not go
looking for it.** If a screen asks you to type something the system could have
worked out, that is a fault worth reporting.

---

## Signing in

Go to the Exceed Box address in a browser, or open the app.

Two ways in:

- **Continue with Google** — your `@exceed-re.ae` account. The normal route.
- **Sign in with email** — for anyone without a Workspace account.

A valid company login is not the same as permission. A new account arrives
switched off, sees nothing, and an admin activates it. If you sign in and
everything is empty, you are not broken — ask an admin to activate you.

## What each role sees

| | Sales | Manager | Admin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your own leads and tasks | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Everyone's leads | No | Yes | Yes |
| Dashboard | No | Yes | Yes |
| Per-person results | No | Yes | Yes |
| Add and activate users | No | No | Yes |

A salesperson genuinely cannot see another salesperson's leads. Not hidden in
the interface, refused by the server. The same is true of business card photos
and voice memos.

There is also a **marketing** role, which sees counts and behaviour but not
phone numbers or email addresses on leads it does not own.

## Today — the screen that matters

Open this first. It lists what to do now, in order, and nothing else.

Each line is one person, why they are on the list, and one button. "Score 92,
looked at the booking page" is the reason; it is there so you never have to
guess why somebody is near the top.

Completing a task takes one tap. The only things you are ever asked to type:

- won, lost, or still in play
- the next contact date
- a voice memo, if you want one

That is deliberate. The last system failed here because it asked for admin.

## Leads

**Finding someone.** Search by name or company. Filters narrow by stage,
region, purpose and owner.

**A lead's page** shows contact details, where they came from, everything they
have done, and their score with the reason for every point.

**Scores** run 0 to 100 and are built from real behaviour:

| What they did | Points |
|---|---|
| Opened an email | 1 |
| Clicked a link | 3 |
| Looked at a landing page | 3 |
| Looked at the booking page | 5 |
| Replied | 10 |
| Booked a consultation | 20 |
| Wants to meet in person | 30 |
| Is a company buying | 30 |
| Wants to be a partner | 30 |
| Has a qualifying budget | 30 |

Behaviour fades. Somebody who clicked in June and went quiet drops back down
and returns to the email sequence rather than sitting on your list for ever.
Facts do not fade: having actually met you is not something that expires.

## Adding people

**A business card.** Photograph it. The details are read on the phone itself,
so it works on bad showroom wifi and costs nothing per scan. Check what it read
before saving. **If a field is blank, that is the system refusing to guess** —
it would rather leave a name empty than invent one. Fill it in yourself.

**A spreadsheet.** Upload it and the importer asks about anything ambiguous
rather than guessing which column is which. You see a preview and the row
errors before anything is saved.

**Somebody already in the system** is matched, not duplicated. One person who
arrived as a card, a spreadsheet row and a web enquiry is one person, and you
can see all three arrivals.

**A referral.** Record who introduced them. Name the introducer even if they
are not in the system. Three introductions from the same person marks them as a
partner automatically.

## The pipeline

Six stages, in order:

New → Nurturing → Engaged → Consultation booked → Negotiating → Won

The first four move by themselves as people react. The last two need a person,
because only you know whether a conversation is really a negotiation.

Move a lead by tapping it and picking the new stage. Every move records who did
it and when.

Separately from the stage, a lead can be marked lost, too early, unreachable or
unsubscribed. These are not stages — someone can be mid-negotiation and
unsubscribed at the same time, and the system has to hold both.

---

## Email

You do not send campaign email. The system does, on a schedule, and stops by
itself when somebody replies, books, or gets warm enough to be worth a person.

One-to-one replies stay in your normal email.

**Somebody who unsubscribes stops receiving mail immediately** — including
anything already queued. That is independent of the sales stage: they may still
be a live deal you call on the phone.

---

## Booking

Customers book themselves, from a link in the email or your website. They pick
a topic and a time, and the system assigns the right person and blocks the slot.

They can cancel or move the appointment from the same link, without calling you.

Times are shown in Japan time. Tokyo is five hours ahead of Dubai, which the
system knows; you should not be doing that arithmetic.

---

## Reporting

The dashboard, for managers and admins: total leads, how many have been mailed,
opens, clicks, replies, bookings, negotiations, deals won, and revenue.

Two things it deliberately will not do:

- **It will not invent a weighted revenue figure.** Until somebody sets the
  chance of closing at each stage, that tile says why it is empty rather than
  showing a number that looks real.
- **It will not double count.** A booking is credited to one source, the first
  one, so the columns add up to the total.

When bookings and negotiations differ, the chart flags the gap. It does not
call it lost business — a meeting can be booked and not yet held.

## When something looks wrong

- **Empty screens after signing in** — your account is not activated yet.
- **A name that is obviously wrong on a scanned card** — edit it. Blanks are on
  purpose, wrong text is worth reporting.
- **A lead you cannot see** — it belongs to somebody else. Ask a manager.
- **A number you do not believe** — every score shows its workings. Open the
  lead and read the breakdown before assuming the total is wrong.
